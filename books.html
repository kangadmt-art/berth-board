How berths are given at the Kandla cargo jetties, as the board applies it. Each rule says where it came from. When a rule changes, it is changed here first and then in the board.

How this book is kept (Mihir, 22 Nov 18:48): every rule is reviewed. A rule that is needed stays. A rule that is of no use, or that a later rule has overridden, is taken out — the latest rule is the final one. What was taken out is listed in section 10 so that it is not brought back by mistake.

0. The one rule — worked out from the record (13 to 23 Nov; Mihir, 22 Nov 18:38: "keep only the rule that is needed")

The port's berthing list is itself laid out by priority. Each priority has a fixed number of places, and every berthed vessel sits under one priority heading.

  • A vessel berths only when a place of her priority falls vacant — the vessel holding it sails. All 31 berthings of the ten days came in this way.
  • The place goes to rank 1 of that priority's column on the list, if the berth suits her.
  • Four things the rank does not decide:

1. Govt — any bulk fertiliser vessel, with or without a rank.

2. HP — the bid. The vessel ranked HP 1 got the berth once in seven.

3. 24 hours — a real 24-hour vessel (a small parcel, often declared at the meeting itself) goes before the big 30K / 25K quote vessels that stand in that column.

4. SAAGAR — bulk, break-bulk (logs) and project cargo are separate lines.

  • Allotment first, berth afterwards (Mihir, 25 Nov 17:02): "Berthing allotment shall be made strictly in accordance with the prescribed order of priority, and the vessel shall thereafter be allocated a suitable available berth, considering its LOA, draft, cargo and operational requirements." So the place belongs to the senior vessel in the order; if the berth in hand does not suit her but another berth of the same meeting window does, the place waits for her there. Only if no berth of the window suits her is the next vessel taken.
  • Berthing goes by the vacant places, in order of priority, first (Mihir, 25 Nov 14:47) — the policy of 24.03.2025 says the same: the vacant berths are assessed in the Order of Allotment, a higher category ahead of a lower one. The vessel then takes the free berth that suits her.
  • A place with no vessel of its own: see section 2. A vacant Govt / Coastal place goes to a Coastal vessel, then the senior 30K quote vessel of the HP column, then SAAGAR (circular 5237 (i) as the port applied it on 26 Nov). A quote place goes to the senior quote vessel (Mihir, 25 Nov: Lila Tokyo was senior; the port had allotted Hai Nam 81 by mistake).
  • Which berth: the new vessel usually takes the berth the last vessel of her own priority left, when it suits her — 8K at CJ-1 every time; Govt at CJ-5 / 6 / 8; SAAGAR at CJ-13 / 15.
  • Salt has berthed under HP, 24 hours, Coastal, SAAGAR and 8K — never under General.

The record: 31 berthings. 21 follow straight from this rule. 5 were HP bids. 5 were settled at the meeting — three real 24-hour vessels and two 30K quote vessels. The sections below are the detail behind it.

0A. The sequence of allotment (note sent by Mihir, 27 Nov 20:02: "Please follow below sequences berthing allotment" — the latest word; transcript in data/2026-11-27/)

The places are filled in this order. When a priority has no vessel, the allotment moves to the next one.

1. Government — 2 berths, by seniority. A geared vessel berths at CJ-1 to CJ-8; an ungeared vessel at CJ-7 / CJ-8.

2. Coastal — 1 berth, by seniority.

3. Higher productivity — 2 berths. Only a vessel whose parcel is 40,000 MT or more qualifies. The ready vessels bid, from 40,000 up to 50,000; the highest bid gets the berth; if all bid 50,000 or the same, seniority decides.

4. SAAGAR — 2 berths: 1 break bulk and 1 bulk, each by seniority. If one kind has no vessel, the other kind may take both.

5. SAAGAR (Project) — 1 vessel at a time, and only against a vacant Government, Coastal or HP place.

6. 30K / 25K / 10K — opens only when a Government berth or the Coastal berth is vacant, and the vessel berths against that vacant place. Vessels ready to do 30,000 go by seniority; if none, those at 25,000; if none, those at 10,000.

7. 24 hours — 1 berth: a vessel that will complete and sail within 24 hours, by seniority. If none, a 30K / 25K / 10K vessel as in 6.

8. 8,000 / 6,500 — 1 berth, by seniority.

9. Steel / Project — 1 berth, by seniority.

10. Bitumen — 1 berth. With no bitumen vessel: a break-bulk vessel ready at 6,500, by seniority.

11. General — 3 berths, the three senior-most vessels. Any berth still vacant after that goes to "Sr. No. 7": a vessel ready to handle norm + 15 %, by seniority.

Who is in the meeting. The meeting is at 11:00 and decides the movements from 18:00 that day to 18:00 the next. A vessel must have arrived and been declared ready by 11:00. An expected vessel is also taken, if she arrives at or before the first high water inside that 18:00–18:00 window; one expected after it waits for the next day's meeting.

Which berth. The group whose vessel leaves gets a berth in her place. If the berth she leaves does not suit the next vessel of that group, that vessel gets another berth falling vacant; if a shift of another vessel makes a suitable berth, the port shifts and berths her. The note's own example: second HP vessel — shift the log vessel from CJ-13 to CJ-5 and give CJ-13; next senior SAAGAR bulk vessel (salt) — shift CJ-14 to CJ-2 and give CJ-14; then the vacant 24-hours place at CJ-15A.

What this changed on the board (v287): a vacant Govt / Coastal place now goes to a SAAGAR project vessel and then a 30K / 25K quote vessel — the earlier reading of circular 5237 (i) (a Coastal vessel, then the SAAGAR line by seniority) is no longer applied; it is an open question to Mihir. The place a SAAGAR bulk vessel leaves goes to the next senior bulk vessel. Not yet in the engine: the 10K step (no list prints a 10K quote yet), the 40,000 MT parcel test for HP, "ungeared Govt vessel at CJ-7 / 8" for vessels of 200 m or less, and norm + 15 % for the left-over berths (the engine gives a left-over berth to the senior General vessel it suits).

1. Priority places (in this order)

  • Government — 2 places. Every bulk fertiliser cargo is Government cargo, even when the list prints no rank. Fertiliser in bags is not. Rock phosphate is not. (Mihir, 20 Nov)
  • Coastal — 1 place.
  • HP goes senior-first among the bidders (Mihir, 27 Nov 10:19); the 1 Nov swap (junior under HP, senior on the 30K slot) is switched off.
  • Higher productivity (HP) — 2 places. HP is a bid (Mihir's team, 21 Nov). Only vessels that can do more than 40,000 MT a day take part. The bid starts at 40,000 and goes up to 50,000 at most. The highest bid gets the berth. If two vessels both bid 50,000, the senior one gets it. So the HP rank on the list does not decide the berth, and the board cannot know the winner before the meeting. The vessels in the HP column of the list are the bidders (Mihir, 27 Nov 10:19: "MSC Magali and other 5 vessels bid for HP and most senior MSC Magali got movement in HP") — the board gives a vacant HP place to the senior-most of them the berth suits. (From 25 to 27 Nov the board gave no berth on an HP number, on his word of 25 Nov that nobody was bidding.) The port shifts another vessel on port convenience to fit an HP bid in (African Hornbill, CJ-16 → CJ-15A). HP time is enforced: a vessel that runs past her quoted time is taken off the berth.
  • SAAGAR — 1 bulk place and 1 break-bulk place. They are two separate priorities. SAAGAR project cargo does not use these two places. (Mihir, 1 and 14 Nov)
  • 24 hours — 1 place. For a vessel that can finish in 24 hours, by seniority and by the berth she needs.
  • 6.5K / 8K — 1 place.
  • Steel / Project — 1 place. By the STL / PRJ column of the list (Mihir, 27 Nov 10:19: Josco Taicang is under Steel as the list prints; Jumbo Kinetic went under Coastal). A vessel ranked in two columns takes whichever place comes first.
  • Bitumen — 1 place.
  • General — 3 places, with at most three bag vessels alongside at a time. (Mihir, 19 Nov)

1A. Timber log norms (Circular 5237 of 21.11.2026)

  • Pine logs: base norm 4,000 a day, whatever the parcel size. Hardwood logs: base norm 5,500 a day.
  • Hardwood is worked out at 5,500 a day, the circular's figure (Mihir, 21 Nov). Pine stays at 4,600, the figure the list prints for a berthed pine vessel (4,000 + 15 %). When the list prints a norm for a vessel, that printed norm is used.
  • Every timber table says which kind she carries — Pine, JAS pine, Hardwood — because the norm depends on it.
  • This is the port's berth norm. Demurrage on the Timber tab still counts 4,000 CBM a day — that is the charter-party basis, a separate thing.

  • A quote vessel works to 30,000 tonnes a day.

2A. When a vessel leaves (Mihir, 22 Nov 18:09)

  • The priority a berthed vessel holds is the one the berthing list prints against her.
  • Like for like. When she leaves, her place goes to the next vessel of the same priority — a 24-hours vessel is replaced by a 24-hours vessel. If the berth she leaves suits that vessel, she takes that berth; if not, she takes the first berth that does suit her, and the berth left behind goes down the order.
  • If that priority has no vessel, the place is given further down (Mihir, 22 Nov 18:09). In the record it went every time to a 30K quote vessel or to project cargo that the free berth suited (section 2).
  • A place is free only once its vessel has sailed. The earlier rule (1 and 7 Nov) — a place whose vessel sails inside the same meeting window is given out again for that window, while she is still alongside — is withdrawn.

3. Seniority

  • General has 3 places. On 27 Nov they are Safi Alfa (1st), Blue Shark (2nd) and Katerina K (3rd); DSM London at CJ-2 stands in the Bitumen place, not in General. (Mihir, 27 Nov 10:19)
  • General goes strictly by seniority — among the vessels the berth suits. A junior does not jump a ready senior who can use the berth. "At most three bag vessels" does not mean skip the bag vessels and take the next one. (Mihir, 20 Nov 17:13) If the free berth does not suit the senior, the berth is still not left empty (Mihir, 22 Nov 17:28): the port shifts vessels to fit her in (section 5), or gives the berth to the senior General vessel it does suit (port's action, 26 Nov: Katerina K, GEN 3, at CJ-6 past GEN 1 and GEN 2; Mihir, 27 Nov: she took the 3rd General place).
  • 6.5K / 8K goes by seniority. A junior does not take the place at a berth her seniors cannot use. (Mihir, 20 Nov 16:50)
  • In the other single-place lines, if the berth does not suit the senior vessel, it goes to the next senior it does suit. (Mihir, 11 Nov)
  • A vessel whose holds are rejected loses her turn and is held until she is ready again. (Mihir, 19 Nov)
  • Government line when the list prints no rank: the vessel that reported first is senior. A vessel still on the expected sheet has not reported, so her ETA counts as her time and she stands behind a vessel that has arrived. (board, 22 Nov — shown to Mihir on the Meeting page draft: Zheng Hui, reported 21 Nov 08:12, before Tai Keystone, still expected for 22 Nov)

4. Which cargo at which berth

  • Clean berths CJ-13, 14, 15, 15A, 16: salt, windmill and project cargo work only here. Fertiliser does not go here.
  • CJ-16: coal, coke, iron ore fines and pellets are allowed at CJ-16, and only there on the clean side. Salt still loads at CJ-16 after coal. (Trade Notice 3089 of 15 Nov; Mihir, 16 Nov)
  • Timber cannot work at CJ-2, 3 or 6.
  • Bags work at CJ-1 to 4 and CJ-13 to 16.
  • "2 shore crane required": bags go to CJ-2 / 3; bulk goes to CJ-6 / 7 / 8 or CJ-13 to 16.
  • Loaded draught: an export vessel is not put at a berth she would have to leave before she is full. The board counts 12.5 m for 40,000 MT and above, 11.2 m for 30,000 MT and above (35,000 MT for bags), otherwise her arrival draught. (the bag step is the board's reading, not yet confirmed)
  • One panel of clear quay is kept between two vessels. (Mihir, 7 Nov)
  • CJ-6 is a damaged berth: nothing may be dumped on it, so only fertiliser vessels work there. (Mihir, 6 Nov)
  • Coal, coke and ore do not go to the bag berths CJ-1 to 4; aggregates do. Aggregates are not dirty cargo (Mihir, 27 Nov 10:19) — they are not counted as non-clean at CJ-16.
  • Ball clay / feldspar normally go to CJ-5 to 8 — the agents ask for these. A clean berth is used only when her list remark asks for it. (Mihir, 31 Oct and 6 Nov; seen again 21 Nov: Gokova-M, 24H 1, was passed over at CJ-15A)
  • Gearless and over 200 m: the shore-crane berths CJ-7 / 8, unless her cargo works only at the clean berths. A gearless vessel of coal or iron ore berths at CJ-16 (Mihir, 25 Nov 17:02; first seen on programme 26.11 — MSC Magali, 253 m, coal, HP(50K)).
  • Only two fertiliser vessels work at a time. (Mihir, 7 Nov)

5. Shifting

  • The port shifts a log or bag vessel off a clean berth when a priority vessel that needs a clean berth is to come in. Such a shift is on the port's account. (Mihir, 31 Oct and 15 Nov)
  • A shift made because an agent wants a particular berth is at that agent's cost.
  • A shift that was programmed and then cancelled is not assumed again until a programme prints it. (Mihir, 20 Nov)
  • The shifted vessel must fit the berth she is moved to. No shift is made for a junior in a line that goes by seniority. (board, 21 Nov)
  • No berth is left vacant while vessels are waiting. Every agent pushes for his vessel and the port does not keep its own jetty empty: it shifts vessels on the port's convenience to fit one in. (Mihir, 22 Nov 17:28)

6. Time at the berth

  • List ETC is the permissible time at the norm the list prints. It is shown as printed.
  • The port programmes a berth only on a declared completion. Programme 28.11 gave no dry-cargo berth and printed no outward row, though three vessels would have finished inside the window by DPR pace or list ETC. The DPR pace says when a berth could come free; the meeting gives it only when the outgoing vessel's completion is declared. (port's action, meeting of 27 Nov)
  • The real finish is counted from the DPR: balance divided by the last 24 hours' work. The DPR's day is not a ceiling; it can be more or less the next day. (Mihir, 11 and 19 Nov) Two cautions (board, 26 Nov): when a later list re-states the vessel's quantity (Safi Alfa, "32100 total"), the DPR balance is out of date and gives no finishing time (noPace); when a programme shifts a working vessel, the hours of the shift are added (lag).
  • A vessel with no norm (project cargo) is carried at her DPR rate once she has a full day on the sheet.
  • Bag vessels under General may stay two days beyond the list ETC. (Mihir, 15 Nov)
  • No outward row means no sailing: a vessel not on the programme or a signal sheet keeps her berth to the end of that programme's window.
  • A vessel is alongside about two hours after the berth is free.
  • A programme's hours are "not later than": the harbour master moves earlier when cargo and tide allow. (seen 21 Nov)

7. Timber laytime and demurrage

  • The first 12 hours after arrival are free. Laytime starts when they end, in every case. (Mihir, 20 Nov)
  • Allowed time = quantity ÷ 4,000 CBM a day.
  • After that, demurrage is USD 20,000 a day until she completes (the DPR's completion hour).
  • Completed before laytime ends = despatch at half the rate, USD 10,000 a day. (Mihir, 16 Nov)
  • Demurrage and despatch are totalled separately, vessel by vessel. Time runs without a break.
  • "Avg dispatch" on the timber sheet means the average discharge per day (CBM a day). (Mihir, 21 Nov)

8. Ship's gear

  • Found online for that vessel: GEARED (with her cranes) or GEARLESS.
  • "(GL)" printed after her name on the DPA list: GEARLESS.
  • Not confirmed and longer than 215 m: MAY BE GEARLESS.
  • Not confirmed and 215 m or less: NO GEAR DETAILS. No guess is made.
  • A tug with barge is gearless.
  • The label is shown wherever a vessel's name appears. (Mihir, final, 21 Nov)

- a strip of the DPA berths — the ones that empty in the meeting's window are marked — and a short list of the berths that continue;

- one card for each berth that empties: Niklegi — the vessel leaving, with the priority the list prints for her; Usi priority ki agli — the next vessels of that same priority, in the list's rank order (HP: the vessels in the bid, no order); Uske baad — the vessels of the priority after it, shown only when the same priority has no vessel the berth suits; Upar ki priority — shown only when a higher priority has a place vacant and a vessel waiting for a berth that suits her.

- The names come straight from the list's rank columns. Whether the board thinks the berth suits a vessel (cargo rule, draught, length) is only a tag on her name — it does not remove her.

- Every vessel carries her details: cargo, quantity, agent, ready since and days waiting (or ETA), length, draught, list ranks; a vessel at berth — cargo, quantity, agent, when she berthed, balance on the latest DPR.

- A vessel whose cargo will run well past her list ETC is shown as continuing, with her list ETC beside her.

- Nothing else is shown there: no "port convenience", no conditions, no shifting notes. After each programme the section is scored on one question — was the vessel that got the berth among the names shown, and in which group.

  • HP bid panel (Mihir, 22 Nov 00:07). HP is decided by a bid at the meeting, so the board does not name an HP winner. Right under the meeting preview it shows who bids at which meeting: every vessel with an HP number or an HP "Y" on the list, in seniority order, with the meeting that programmes the first berth she can use once it is clear (a vessel lying across two berths blocks both; a berth more than 1 m short of her draft is left out), the last quote that won HP for the same cargo, and where the board expects her otherwise. Below it: the winning quotes so far, cargo-wise, and the full record (HP_LOG + the HP vessels still in V; trim_board.js moves a vessel into HP_LOG when it drops her). An HP pick in the meeting preview carries the tag "HP · decided by bid". The programme prints only the winner's quote — the losing bids come only from the team after the meeting.

9. Which paper wins when two disagree

  • Harbour master's signal sheet, then the programme, then the berthing list, then the DPR.
  • Mihir's word is above all of them.
  • The port is taken as right. A mismatch is kept as an open point until Mihir confirms.
  • On a signal sheet, only a struck row is cancelled. A row that is merely missing is kept and asked about.
  • Team's timber desk sheet vs the port list (Mihir, 21 Nov): the team's message is corrected from the port's papers. Where the two differ (arrival hour, berthing hour, ETA, quantity), the DPA berthing list is right. The desk sheet is used only for what the list does not print (ETB dates, port of loading, vessels not yet on the list).
  • Ready or not ready: a vessel is ready only when her agent declares it and the berthing list shows it. A rank printed on the list is not readiness.
  • The team's word of a hold vs a later list (Mihir, 27 Nov 10:19: "please go through the berthing list"): the berthing list decides. If a later list prints the vessel ready and ranked, the board follows the list.
  • Vessel names: the port's list can carry typing errors ("Paiwan Ice" for Paiwan Ace). The team's name is used; the list spelling is kept in the note.

Circulars and trade notices on record

What is done with each paper, step by step. The aim: papers go into the data book, the rule book is applied, and the display board shows the result.

1. What Mihir does

  • Send the papers in the chat as they come: berthing list, DPR, programme, signal sheets, timber desk sheet, circulars. No text is needed.
  • Say in one line when a rule changes or the port does something different.
  • Ask any question. The answer is taken from the data book.

2. Daily berthing list (Excel, position 0700)

  • At-berth sheet: who is alongside, panels, made fast, commenced, norm, list ETC.
  • Waiting sheet: every waiting vessel with her ranks, readiness and remarks. "B'TODAY" and an X in the rank columns mean she is being berthed.
  • Expected sheet: ETA of vessels not yet arrived.
  • New vessel: add her record, and look up her gear once.
  • A vessel that has left the list has sailed; her exact hour comes from the DPR.

3. Daily performance report — DPR (position 0700)

  • When a later list re-states a vessel's quantity, the DPR balance is out of date: no finishing time is taken from it until the next DPR. When a programme shifts a working vessel, the hours of the shift are added to her finish. (board, 26 Nov — Safi Alfa; Asian Summit, Blue Shark)
  • The DPR's "Stevd." column gives the stevedore of every working vessel. After each DPR the board's stevedore list is rebuilt from all the DPRs on file and shown beside the agent (Mihir, 25 Nov).
  • For each vessel: work in the last 24 hours, total so far, balance, gangs and hooks.
  • Remarks give the actual hours: made fast, commenced, completed, left the berth.
  • Midstream and mooring rows go to the barge record.
  • A vessel that worked only part of the day is marked so; her figure is not a day's rate.

4. Movement programme (after the 11:00 meeting)

  • Who berths and who sails in the window 18:00 to 18:00.
  • The board's call for that meeting is scored against it: named right, berth right.
  • The call is never rebuilt after the meeting hour.

5. Harbour master's signal sheets (photos)

  • Each row: pilot, hour, IN / OUT / SHIFT, vessel, from, to.
  • For an IN from OTB the hour is the pilot boarding; she is alongside about two hours later.
  • A sheet can have two pages. An amended sheet replaces the earlier one.

6. Timber desk sheet

  • CBM, origin, ETA and expected berthing date of every log vessel.
  • Laytime and demurrage are counted on the desk's CBM.

7. After every paper

  • The record of each vessel in the data book is updated: nothing is deleted; a sailed vessel stays in the record.
  • The rule book is applied to give the next berth and date for every ready vessel.
  • If the result for a Mihir & Company vessel moves, it is said plainly with the reason.
  • Checks are run before anything is published.
  • The display board is published, and the data book is rebuilt from it with one command: node tools/build_db.js <board> then python3 tools/build_books.py.

8. Asking the data book

  • Examples: average waiting of salt vessels; every call at CJ-13 this month; how many days a vessel took against her norm; what was predicted and what happened.
  • The answer is worked out from the data book files, not from memory.

9. Where things are kept

  • Data book: db/databook.json in the repository, and the Data book page.
  • Rule book and process book: book/ in the repository, and the same page.
  • Every paper received: data/YYYY-MM-DD/ in the repository.
  • Display board: the DPA Berth Board page.

Header of the board (from 21 Nov)

  • Under the title: "Board owned by @mk", the last-updated time, and one small box per paper — List, Programme, DPR, Signals — with the date the board is updated to.
  • "Sources & tide" holds one sentence naming the day's papers, and the tide. The full log of every paper received is kept in handover/SOURCES_LOG.md, newest first; add a line there with every update.
